# Presentation Design

Use this skill only after factual content has passed the research evidence gate.

1. Start from audience, purpose, decision, and time available.
2. Give each slide one primary message. Use a clear title and a small number of visible evidence-backed points.
3. Prefer charts or tables only when they reduce cognitive effort; do not decorate weak evidence with visual complexity.
4. Keep verified facts, inference, proposals, risks, and unresolved items visually distinguishable.
5. Preserve source lineage in notes or appendices for factual claims.
6. Use consistent typography, spacing, alignment, and reading order. Prioritize accessibility and legibility over novelty.
7. Never invent a statistic, source, logo, customer, benchmark, or product capability to improve the story.
8. This skill grants no external design-service, asset-download, or upload authority.
